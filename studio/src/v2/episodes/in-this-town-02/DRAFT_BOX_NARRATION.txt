In this town, if a cat stays inside a box too long... they become the box. And nobody knows how to turn them back.

Mocha never liked boxes. But Latte loved his striped box beside the café window. And Essie slept in her cardboard box every night.

One morning, Mocha returned to open the café. Latte was gone. His favorite striped box was still beneath the window.

"Latte?"

Mocha lifted the flaps. A tiny, familiar meow came from inside the cardboard.

Latte hadn't left. He had become the box.

Mocha tried food. He shook the box gently. He begged it to move. Nothing worked.

From then on, Mocha watched Essie constantly. Every time she climbed into her favorite box, he pulled her out. Essie didn't understand. She just wanted somewhere safe to sleep.

Then one night, Mocha finished closing the café. Essie hadn't come home.

He looked at her usual sleeping spot. Her box was gone too.

Mocha ran outside. He checked the bus stop. The empty market. Every alley he could find.

Finally, beneath a streetlamp, he saw Essie dragging her cardboard box toward a quiet corner.

"Essie! Wait!"

But she was too far away.

She curled up inside. Mocha raced down the alley.

By the time he reached her, the change had begun.

Her paws grew stiff. Her whiskers faded. And right before his eyes... Essie folded into a cardboard box.

Mocha froze. First Latte. Now Essie.

He tapped the box. Whispered her name. Held it close. Still nothing.

The alley turned cold.

Mocha looked inside. He'd never understood why his friends loved boxes.

But Essie's box was warm. Dry. A place where she had always felt safe.

And now, she was trapped inside it.

Mocha had never climbed into a box before. But he couldn't leave her alone.

So he climbed in.

The cardboard shook. Two ears appeared. And Essie tumbled into his arms.

She was back.

Mocha had found the cure.

Together, they raced to Latte's striped box. Essie held the flaps open. Mocha climbed inside.

Pop!

Latte sprang out, knocking them both over.

The next morning, Mocha told the entire town what he'd discovered:

"If someone becomes a box... climb inside with them. They'll come back."

And after that, Mocha still didn't love boxes.

But he kept one by the café window.

Big enough for all three of them.
