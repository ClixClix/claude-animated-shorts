In this town, every night at exactly eleven eleven, the little café door opened. No one could see who opened it. And nobody knew why.

Most cats avoided that street after dark. But Latte was especially afraid. He counted the nine streetlights between the café and his house. If they were all glowing, he felt safe.

One rainy night, Latte stayed out too late.

The first streetlight blinked. Then the second went out. Then a third.

Latte ran.

He splashed past the empty bus stop, slipped around a puddle, and reached his porch just as the last light went dark.

Inside, he hit the switch. Click. Nothing.

The entire street had lost power.

Except for one building.

Across the road, the café still glowed. Latte stared through his window as the clock reached eleven eleven.

The café door swung open.

Then came three tiny taps.

Tap. Tap. Tap.

Latte pulled his blanket over his head. Whatever was out there could stay out there.

But then he heard a meow. So small he almost missed it beneath the rain.

He looked again. Behind the foggy café window, an enormous shadow rose. Two pointed ears. Something long dragged across the floor.

It moved toward the door.

Latte's paws shook. He wanted to hide. But the tiny meow came again, and this time he crossed the street.

At the café door, he saw what had been making the taps.

A soaking-wet kitten. Too little to reach the handle.

The enormous shadow rose behind her.

Latte squeezed his eyes shut.

When he opened them, there was Mocha, struggling with a blanket almost twice his size.

Essie brought fresh water. Mocha laid the blanket by the door. The kitten stepped inside, and Latte followed.

Beside a flowerpot, Latte noticed a little sign. It said: If you're scared of the dark, come inside.

Mocha opened the café every night at eleven eleven, just after the last bus left, in case someone had nowhere to go.

He wasn't waiting for customers.

He was leaving the light on for anyone who needed it.

Latte sat beside the kitten. The street stayed dark. But for the first time, neither of them was afraid.
